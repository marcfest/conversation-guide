Conversation Guide

Four questions to prepare, listen, and respond.

  • What do you think?
  • What has been your experience?
  • What does that mean to you?
  • What would help?
Prepare beforehand — answer the four yourself
→
Listen ask them the four, to understand
→
Respond answer the four to make your point

These four questions spark a conversation — they don't script the whole thing. Ask them to understand the other person. Then answer the same four to make your own point. Go in order, and don't rush: stay with each question, and ask a follow-up before moving on.

Your mental movie

The scene you invented to remember the four questions.

Prepare

Readiness, not a script — light notes you'll draw on flexibly.

What do you think?

Your main point, in one clear sentence.

What has been your experience?

Get concrete. Specifics let the other person "see" what you mean. Reach for: a time ("last Tuesday," not "recently"), a place ("our downtown clinic," not "our location"), a number with context ("374 families," not "hundreds"), a name, a quote, or a visual detail. Drop two or three of these concrete "nuggets" rather than telling a long story.

What does that mean to you?

Why it matters to you — the significance behind your point.

What would help?

What you'd suggest, or what you hope happens next. Keep it open, not a demand.
